R1 — NBA and NHL stay paused until their CLV turns

Both sports were alerting and losing: NBA 0 for 11 paper bets (−$45), NHL 4 for 15 against 6.7 expected (−$46). That is $91 of the $104 lost. The detector's own measurement agrees that this isn't bad luck. NBA's opportunities average −3.40% at the close (90% interval −7.85% to +0.59%, 31 opportunities on 17 games) and beat it 42% of the time. NHL's average +0.25% (−1.64% to +2.00%) and beat it 46% of the time. A book's own margin is about 2–3%, so an "edge" that ends up at zero is the margin being paid.

Both sports are still polled and measured: the setting stops alerts, not data. Unpause a sport when its opportunity CLV is positive with at least 50 measured, and its interval no longer reaches below zero.

Edge retention, the closing value as a share of the edge detected, tells the same story for football moneylines. Edges of 2–3% keep −36%, so they reverse. 3–5% keep 142% and 5–10% keep 122%: the market moves further toward us than the detector thought. 10%+ keep 80%. Above 3%, a detected edge is a real edge.

Effect: in football the threshold would keep 187 of 261 opportunities, at +8.86% mean CLV with 80% beating the close, and drop 74 that average −0.77%. Volume falls by about a quarter from what the per-game cooldown would send. That still leaves well over the old rule's one-per-sport trickle.

The counterpoint, stated plainly: the graded football bets split the other way. The 13 with edges under 3% are 5 for 13 and +$3.14; the 35 at 3% and up are 5 for 35 and −$18.75. Those 35 are mostly long shots, though, and football as a whole has won 10 bets against 10.1 expected. Results on 48 bets mostly at +300 and longer are noise. Closing value is the better estimate, and it is not close: −0.77% against +8.86%.

min_edge_to_bet_pct (1.5) sits below the threshold, so it does nothing either way. Leave it.

R4 — Books: no change, but DraftKings is the one to watch

When the book offering the price is DraftKings, the edge rarely holds: +1.59% in football (interval −0.88% to +4.33%, n=20, beat 55%), against +5.45% to +8.73% for every other book. That fits DraftKings being the sharpest of the seven: a DraftKings price that disagrees with the others is more often the others lagging than DraftKings being stale. Disabling DraftKings would also remove it from the consensus, which needs five books, so that is the wrong lever. A "never bet at this book" setting would need code, and 20 opportunities don't justify it yet. Revisit at 50.

BetRivers, Bally Bet and betPARX look best (about +6.2% each, 80% beat). They share one pricing feed (Kambi), though, so their 124 rows are one source of edge counted three times.

T4.4b is met as written: 67 recommended opportunities with a clean CLV average +6.03% (+2.18% to +10.47%), against +4.34% for the 253 not recommended. That is positive, and not below the other side. But those 67 were chosen by the old one-alert-per-sport cooldown, which always picked the single biggest edge. The rules changed this week: per-game alerting on 11-01, the NBA and NHL pause on 11-04, and R2 if accepted. The recommendations from here on are a different selection, and it hasn't been measured. Two weeks of paper under the new rules should produce roughly 50 new recommendations. If their CLV is positive and close to the opportunity side, the evidence for going live is about as strong as this sample allows.

Turning paper_mode off stays your decision alone (§16.2); nothing here will do it.

2. What CLV can and can't tell you before real money

  • The "close" is a consensus of seven Maryland retail books. There's no sharp book among them (Pinnacle and Circa aren't available here), so beating a soft consensus is a weaker claim than beating a sharp close. The +6% is likely an overstatement of the true edge, by an unknown amount.
  • A paper price is not a filled bet. The alert has to reach you and you have to place it before the line moves. The soft books that supply most of the edge (the Kambi three, FanDuel) also limit accounts that win. Expect some stakes to be cut or refused.
  • The dollar record will mostly be variance. The median recommendation is about +375, which wins about one time in four. At the current caps ($1,000 bankroll, at most 2% a bet), a month's P&L will swing by more than its expected gain. Judge the system by CLV, weekly, not by its balance.
  • Weeks vary. Football's weekly CLV ran +4.7% to +10.4% for four weeks, then −1.07% in W43 (10-19 to 10-25), then +5.03% in W44. One bad week is normal; two in a row would be a signal.

4. Method

  • Source: edgeline-opportunities, every type: ev document with a stored clv_pct, through 2026-11-04: 394 +EV opportunities, 320 measured. 53 circular measurements (the closing price was the detecting poll's own) and 21 not yet measurable are left out, as python -m edgeline.clv does.
  • CLV is §12's clv_pct: the closing consensus fair probability times the price at first detection (or at the alert, where there was one), minus one. It's the expected return if the close is right.
  • Intervals are 90% cluster bootstraps over games, 4,000 draws. Several opportunities on one game (three books with one price, or both sides of a market) aren't independent evidence, so the game is the unit resampled.
  • Paper results are joined result → recommendation → opportunity. "Expected" wins use the detector's own probability, (1 + edge) / price.
  • Reproduce with python -m edgeline.clv for the top-line split. The cuts here came from one-off scripts over the same documents.
